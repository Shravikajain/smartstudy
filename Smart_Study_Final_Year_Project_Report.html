ABSTRACT

Students preparing for examinations often have to locate recurring topics across past papers, create additional practice questions and judge what to study next. Smart Study is a web-based learning application that brings these activities into one workflow. A student organizes work by subject and topic, uploads past examination papers in PDF format, and uses extracted text to identify question themes and recurring questions. The application can generate original multiple-choice or subjective practice questions from a selected topic and available study material. Its quiz service records responses and adjusts an ability estimate so that the next question is selected near the learner’s current estimated level. Subjective responses can receive model-assisted scores and feedback. A separate interview-practice workflow supports academic-subject or engineering-role sessions, typed responses and browser-recorded speech transcribed by a local Whisper model in the ML container. The system is implemented as a browser frontend, a Spring Boot API, a Python FastAPI machine-learning service and a PostgreSQL database, deployed together with Docker Compose. This report presents the problem, related learning systems, requirements, design, data model, implementation and test evidence. The current repository includes automated tests for core quiz, analysis, PDF and transcription behavior; their results and the limits of the available evidence are reported explicitly. Smart Study is a practice and revision aid, not a replacement for instructor judgment or an accredited examination platform. Its usefulness depends on the quality of uploaded material and generated feedback, and generated content should be reviewed by learners and instructors.

Keywords: adaptive learning , examination preparation, question generation, item response theory, past-paper analysis, interview practice.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION

1.1 Overview and Issues Involved

Past examination papers are valuable because they expose learners to the wording, topic emphasis and level of questions used in earlier assessments. In practice, however, a student may have to collect papers from several locations, read them manually, notice repeated concepts, prepare a personal question bank and then decide which question to attempt next. These steps are time-consuming and do not automatically provide targeted feedback. A static list of questions also gives every learner the same sequence even when their knowledge differs.

Smart Study addresses this workflow through a browser application organized around subjects and topics. Users can maintain their own subject areas, upload past papers, inspect recurring themes, generate new practice questions, take a quiz that selects questions according to an evolving ability estimate, and rehearse academic or engineering interviews. The system combines deterministic software components (storage, parsing, topic organization, quiz state and ability updates) with machine-learning services for text analysis, question generation, written feedback and speech transcription.

The application is designed as a study aid. Text extraction may be imperfect for scanned or poorly formatted documents; semantic clustering can group questions that share vocabulary without being conceptually identical; and generated questions or scores may contain errors. Consequently, the application should present its outputs as guidance and preserve the learner’s responsibility to verify educational content.

1.2 Problem Definition

University learners lack a single, account-based workflow that converts their own past-paper material into organized, recurring-topic insights and adaptive practice while also supporting written-answer feedback and interview rehearsal. Manual analysis is repetitive; static practice does not adapt to demonstrated performance; and separate tools often divide content management, practice and feedback across different interfaces.

Project objective. Design and implement a deployable web system that securely organizes study content, extracts and analyzes uploaded examination papers, generates topic-linked practice, adapts quiz difficulty from responses, and supports conversational interview practice with optional voice input.

Project objectives

  • Provide account registration, login and user-scoped access to subjects and study material.
  • Allow learners to create subjects/topics and upload PDF question papers with an optional examination year.
  • Extract text from digital PDFs and route scanned documents to a protected OCR service.
  • Group extracted questions and report recurring questions and topic clusters across papers.
  • Generate original multiple-choice and subjective questions and conduct adaptive quizzes.
  • Provide rubric-guided feedback for subjective answers and conduct subject/role interview sessions.
  • Support browser audio capture and local-service transcription without sending interview audio to an external speech provider.

Scope and constraints

The implemented scope is a single-page browser interface, Spring Boot REST API, FastAPI ML service and PostgreSQL-backed storage, with Docker Compose as the local deployment route. The ML text-generation integration uses an Ollama-compatible endpoint configured through environment variables. Speech recognition uses Faster-Whisper in the ML container. A working provider key and configured services are required for model-backed features. No claim is made that the model outputs are guaranteed correct, that the system is a high-stakes assessment tool, or that the current repository represents a production-scale hosted service.

1.3 Proposed Solution

Smart Study uses a service-oriented design. The frontend presents sign-in, subject dashboards, paper management, practice and interview views. The backend authenticates requests, checks ownership, persists domain data and coordinates calls to the ML service. The ML service exposes typed HTTP endpoints for analysis, question generation, grading, interview turns, scanned-PDF OCR and audio transcription. PostgreSQL stores structured entities; uploaded PDFs are kept in a persistent uploads volume. The system is composed with Docker so that its service boundaries and dependencies can be started together.

The paper-analysis pipeline extracts question-like text, normalizes vocabulary, creates TF-IDF features and clusters questions using K-means. Candidate cluster counts are compared using the silhouette score within a configured bound. Similar questions across different papers are joined using cosine similarity and a configurable threshold; repeated concepts are returned with occurrence and year metadata. This produces an inspection aid rather than a guarantee of syllabus coverage.

Adaptive practice maintains an ability estimate for a quiz session. The implementation uses a logistic response probability and a bounded update after each answer, then selects a remaining question whose difficulty is closest to the current estimate. This is a simplified item-response-inspired procedure; it is not a calibrated psychometric instrument and does not estimate all parameters of a full item response model.

Figure 1.1: High-level study workflow. Generated and analyzed material returns to a learner-controlled practice and review loop.

Expected contribution

The project’s contribution is the integration of paper organization, recurring-question analysis, question generation, adaptive practice and interview rehearsal in one subject-centered application. Compared with static question lists, the quiz loop responds to prior answers; compared with standalone model prompts, the workflow stores user-owned topics, papers, questions and quiz sessions in a structured application. These are design-level advantages; comparative learning outcomes have not been established by a controlled educational study.

CHAPTER 2
LITERATURE SURVEY

2.1 Existing Solutions

Digital learning products commonly provide some combination of structured lessons, flashcards, assessment authoring, question banks and progress tracking. The following review compares representative tools and categories relevant to Smart Study. It is a functional comparison based on publicly described product capabilities, not a benchmark or independent evaluation of learning effectiveness.

2.1.1 Learning management systems and Moodle Quiz

Moodle is an open-source learning management system used to deliver course content, activities and assessments. Its Quiz activity supports instructor-authored questions, attempts, grading and feedback within a course [6]. It is a capable platform when an institution configures the course and question bank. Smart Study differs in its focus on an individual learner’s past examination papers, automatic cross-paper topic/repetition analysis and adaptive practice workflow. Moodle’s extensive administration model may be more than a learner needs for personal revision.

2.1.2 Quizlet

Quizlet centers on user- or teacher-created study sets and offers digital flashcard and practice experiences [7]. Its strength is quick vocabulary and concept review supported by a large content ecosystem. A user typically needs prepared study material; it does not constitute the project’s specific pipeline for parsing a learner’s examination-paper collection, clustering paper questions and using those patterns to seed topic-linked adaptive quiz sessions.

2.1.3 Khan Academy

Khan Academy provides structured lessons and practice across supported subjects, with learner progress linked to a curated curriculum [8]. Its strength is the combination of instructional material and exercises. Smart Study is instead designed to start from a learner’s own examination papers and topic organization. It does not attempt to reproduce Khan Academy’s broad curriculum, instructional video library or institution-scale learning analytics.

2.1.4 Commercial exam-preparation platforms

Exam-preparation services commonly provide syllabus-aligned courses, mock examinations, question banks and performance reports for particular examinations. Their curated content can provide consistency and exam-specific guidance. Their scope is often tied to a provider’s content catalog and target assessment. Smart Study’s intended distinction is learner-supplied paper analysis and customizable subjects rather than a centrally curated, examination-specific question bank.

2.1.5 AI study assistants and question generators

General-purpose AI assistants and educational question generators can create explanations, practice prompts and conversational feedback. They offer flexible interaction but depend on the supplied context and the model’s reliability. A standalone chat does not automatically provide a persistent relation between a learner’s subjects, uploaded papers, generated question records, quiz state and past-paper recurrence. Smart Study wraps model services in a structured subject-and-quiz workflow and validates generated question fields before storing them.

2.1.6 Speech-enabled interview practice

Interview simulators and voice assistants can help candidates rehearse responses. Smart Study’s interview workflow supports subject or engineering-role targets, question sequences and feedback; the browser can capture audio and submit it to the local ML service for transcription. This is a focused practice workflow, not a hiring assessment, professional interview credential or guarantee of interview performance.

Research concepts informing the project

Item response theory models the probability of a response in relation to learner ability and item properties. The project borrows the intuition that practice can be selected near an evolving estimate of ability, while implementing a deliberately simpler bounded logistic update [1, 2]. TF-IDF assigns greater importance to terms that are informative within a document collection, while K-means groups vector representations around centroids [3, 4]. The silhouette coefficient can help compare candidate clusterings by considering compactness and separation [5]. These techniques are used as practical analysis components; none alone understands educational semantics or proves that a cluster is pedagogically coherent.

Gap and justification. The reviewed systems typically emphasize curated courses, flashcard sets, instructor-managed assessments or open-ended generative chat. The project addresses a narrower integration gap: a learner can bring their own paper corpus, organize it by subject, inspect recurring patterns and continue directly into generated and adaptive practice, with interview rehearsal in the same application. The gap is functional and architectural; user studies are still required to demonstrate learning benefit.

2.2 Methodology and Conceptual Requirements

2.2.1 Development methodology

The project follows an iterative implementation approach. First, the repository’s user-facing workflow and domain objects are defined. Next, API contracts and persistence entities are implemented in the Spring Boot service. The Python service supplies analysis and model-backed tasks behind typed request/response schemas. The browser interface consumes the API, while Docker Compose connects the application and database. Automated unit, integration and API tests are used to check important behaviors. Iterations address the paper-import path, adaptive quiz lifecycle, interview turn flow and voice transcription. Configuration values and secrets are supplied through environment variables rather than committed source.

2.2.2 Concepts and algorithms

  • REST service: An HTTP API exposes resources and actions using JSON or multipart form data. The frontend communicates with the backend; the backend calls the internal ML service.
  • PDF extraction and OCR: PDFBox extracts embedded text from digitally generated documents. When scanned pages contain no usable text, an authenticated internal endpoint can render pages and run OCR. Extraction quality depends on document clarity and language support.
  • TF-IDF: Each question is represented by weighted one- and two-word terms. Frequent general examination words are removed to reduce uninformative similarity.
  • K-means and silhouette selection: K-means groups the sparse text vectors. The analysis chooses a cluster count by checking silhouette scores for a bounded set of candidates; deterministic random seeds make the process more reproducible.
  • Cosine similarity and repeated questions: Questions above a similarity threshold are linked only across different paper IDs. Connected groups supply the repeated-question list and years.
  • Adaptive quiz: A logistic probability estimates the chance of a correct response given ability and difficulty. After an answer, the estimate is updated by a diminishing step and clamped to a supported range. The next eligible item is the one closest to the current estimate.
  • Structured generation and feedback: The ML service requests schema-constrained JSON and validates question type, option count, correct-answer index and reference-answer requirements. Subjective feedback is model-assisted and should be treated as formative feedback.
  • Speech transcription: The browser records an audio response and sends it to the internal API. Faster-Whisper performs local CPU transcription in the ML container; the resulting text is submitted as the candidate response.

2.2.3 Security and privacy considerations

Authenticated application routes use JWT-based identity and user ownership checks. Internal OCR/transcription calls are protected by a shared service key and constant-time comparison. Deployment secrets, database credentials and model-provider keys are configured externally. Uploaded papers and audio contain potentially sensitive information, so operators should restrict access to volumes, use HTTPS outside localhost, establish retention rules, and avoid uploading material without the right to use it. Model providers may process text sent for generation or grading; operators must review provider terms and avoid sensitive personal data. The local transcription path is designed not to send captured interview audio to an external speech provider.

3.2 Hardware Requirements

Development/deployment host (minimum practical baseline): 64-bit Windows, macOS or Linux capable of running Docker Desktop/Engine; a multi-core CPU; 8 GB RAM for a small local trial (16 GB or more recommended when PostgreSQL, the Java service and the local speech model run together); several GB of free disk for images, uploaded papers and the downloaded Whisper model; and a microphone for voice practice. Client device: A recent laptop, desktop or mobile device with a standards-compliant browser and network access to the application. Microphone access requires a secure context such as localhost or HTTPS and explicit browser permission. If the client microphone is unavailable, interview answers can be typed.

External services: The configured Ollama endpoint and model must be reachable for question generation, grading and conversational interview responses. When using a hosted provider, a valid API key is required. These are operational dependencies, not hardware requirements of the learner’s browser.

3.3.2 Use Case Descriptions (Rational Unified Process Style)

UC-01 — Register or Sign In

Primary actor: Learner. Preconditions: Application is reachable; learner has a valid email and password for the selected action. Trigger: Learner submits the registration or login form. Main flow: (1) System validates required fields. (2) For registration, the service creates the account if the email is not already in use; for login, it verifies credentials. (3) On success, the service issues an authentication token and the client opens the subject dashboard. Alternatives: Invalid fields, duplicate email or rejected credentials return an error and leave the user unauthenticated. Postconditions: Successful authentication establishes the user context for subsequent requests.

UC-02 — Create Subject and Topic

Primary actor: Authenticated learner. Preconditions: Valid authenticated session. Trigger: Learner submits a subject or topic form. Main flow: (1) Client sends the name and related parent identifier. (2) Backend validates the request and confirms parent ownership. (3) Backend stores the new record and returns it to the client. Alternatives: Missing/invalid names or a resource owned by another user are rejected. Postconditions: The subject/topic is available within that learner’s workspace.

UC-03 — Upload and Analyze Past Papers

Primary actor: Authenticated learner. Preconditions: Learner owns a subject; input is a supported PDF within configured upload limits. Trigger: Learner uploads a paper and optionally supplies its year. Main flow: (1) Backend verifies subject ownership and stores the file under a generated name. (2) Text is extracted from the PDF; if the normal extraction path yields no usable text, the configured OCR flow may be used. (3) Paper metadata and text are associated with the subject. (4) On analysis request, extracted questions are normalized, vectorized, clustered and compared across papers. (5) Cluster and recurrence results are returned. Alternatives: Invalid PDF, unreadable scan, no papers or unavailable ML service produce an explicit error. Failed upload processing removes the newly stored file where possible. Postconditions: A successful upload is attached to its subject; analysis is returned without changing the source paper.

UC-04 — Generate Practice Questions

Primary actor: Authenticated learner. Preconditions: Learner owns a subject and has selected one or more topics. Trigger: Learner requests a practice set. Main flow: (1) Backend composes a typed generation request with subject, topics, requested question type and optional paper context. (2) ML service requests structured output from the configured language model. (3) Response fields and question-specific rules are validated. (4) Usable questions are stored against their topic and returned. Alternatives: Provider unavailable, malformed output or no valid questions are reported as failure; invalid model items are not silently presented as questions. Postconditions: Valid questions are available for later practice.

UC-05 — Complete an Adaptive Quiz

Primary actor: Authenticated learner. Preconditions: Learner owns the subject; a usable question pool exists. Trigger: Learner starts a quiz. Main flow: (1) Backend creates a quiz session and selects the initial question. (2) Learner submits an answer. (3) Backend records correctness, updates ability and answer counts, and chooses the next question closest to current ability. (4) System returns answer feedback and progress. (5) Session ends at its configured maximum or when no questions remain. Alternatives: An invalid session/question/answer is rejected; subjective answers are submitted to the grading service and may be unavailable if that dependency is down. Postconditions: Completed session records its status, counts and final estimate.

UC-06 — Practice an Interview

Primary actor: Authenticated learner. Preconditions: Interview service and, for voice, browser microphone permission are available. Trigger: Learner chooses a subject or engineering role, level and question count. Main flow: (1) Client requests the next interview turn with prior conversation context. (2) ML service returns a question. (3) Learner types or records an answer. (4) Recorded audio is sent through the protected transcription route; transcription text becomes the answer. (5) Service evaluates the answer and returns feedback/score or the next question. (6) Session completes at the selected limit. Alternatives: Microphone denial or transcription failure leaves text entry available; service errors are displayed. Postconditions: Learner receives session feedback; browser audio is not sent to an external speech provider by this design.

4.1 Database Design

The backend uses JPA entities mapped to relational tables. The following ER view summarizes the core relations implemented in the domain model. A learner owns subjects; subjects contain topics and uploaded papers; topics contain questions; quiz sessions refer to a learner and subject and maintain a question pool; answers link a session to the question answered. Question options are persisted as an ordered element collection. The many-to-many question pool is represented by a join table.

CHAPTER 5
CONCLUSION

5.1 Conclusion

Smart Study demonstrates an integrated web application for organizing examination preparation around learner-supplied subject material. Its implemented workflow connects account-scoped subjects and topics, PDF paper ingestion, question and recurrence analysis, generated practice, adaptive quiz sessions, subjective feedback and interview rehearsal. A Java API coordinates persistent domain operations, while a Python ML service isolates text analysis, language-model interaction, OCR and speech transcription. Docker Compose describes a repeatable local multi-service deployment.

The project also demonstrates how a simple ability estimate can influence practice selection. The implemented logistic update and nearest-difficulty selection make quiz sessions responsive to learner answers, while remaining intentionally simpler than a fully calibrated item-response assessment. Automated Python tests executed for this report passed 35 tests. Existing backend test reports record 37 passing tests across six suites, but the fresh backend test command could not run in the available environment because of JVM native-memory allocation failure. These results support important implementation paths; they do not establish educational effectiveness, model accuracy, accessibility compliance, load capacity or production readiness.

The main practical value is workflow integration rather than a claim that automated generation or clustering is inherently superior to instructor-authored learning. Uploaded paper quality, correct user configuration, reliable model responses, review of generated items and secure operations remain necessary. Before institutional submission or broader use, the team should complete real-browser screenshots, verify the full deployment with its approved provider configuration, add explicit authorization and failure-path tests, inspect model output quality with educators, and update this document with authentic student, guide and plagiarism-check details.

5.2 Limitations

  • TF-IDF and lexical similarity may miss paraphrases or create false matches; cluster labels are descriptive terms, not validated syllabus topics.
  • Question generation and subjective grading depend on an external language model and can be incorrect, inconsistent or biased.
  • The adaptive algorithm is a simplified pedagogical heuristic; it has not been calibrated on a representative learner/item dataset.
  • OCR accuracy varies with scan quality, layout and language; extraction should be inspected before relying on results.
  • No controlled learner study, formal accessibility review, production load test or availability evaluation is included in the current evidence.
  • The current local deployment requires operator-provided secrets, database setup and model-service configuration.

5.3 Future Scope

  • Conduct educator and learner evaluation; measure task completion, usefulness, question quality and learning outcomes.
  • Add instructor review/approval workflows and citations to the uploaded source passages behind generated questions.
  • Improve semantic matching with embedding-based retrieval, while retaining transparent thresholds and human review.
  • Evaluate calibrated IRT models using sufficient response data, item calibration, uncertainty estimates and privacy-preserving analytics.
  • Add versioned database migrations, retention controls, backups, accessibility testing, security assessment and operational monitoring.
  • Support more document types and languages after measuring extraction accuracy and defining data-handling requirements.
